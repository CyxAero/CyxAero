# Christine Mmanam

I care about the space between a person and their screen - that moment where something just works, looks right, and feels like it was made for them. That’s what I build towards.

My background is in frontend development and UI/UX design, but what actually drives me is something harder to put a label on: the belief that software can have a soul. That an app can be something people genuinely love, not just use. I’m drawn to interfaces that are calm, considered, and a little bit joyful; whether that’s a mobile app, a desktop tool, or something that doesn’t fit neatly into a category yet.

Right now, I’m learning Rust because I want to build things that are beautiful and honest, performant, offline-first, with no unnecessary complexity hidden behind abstractions I don’t understand. I want to know why every piece is there.

I’m a final-year BSc Information Technology student at Middlesex University London, figuring out what kind of developer I want to be. The answer, so far: one who makes good things feel simple.



## What I’m working on

Inktome (in progress) - a privacy-first, offline-first book tracking app in Flutter. Custom navigation, a full three-layer architecture, and a design system built from scratch. A labour of love.



## What I’m learning

- Rust — working through the fundamentals, building toward native desktop apps
- Kotlin / Android development — exploring mobile engineering beyond Flutter
- The craft of making complex things feel effortless.



## Things I believe about software

Good design isn’t decoration; It’s the difference between something that works and something that lands.

Offline-first isn’t a constraint but a form of respect for the user.

Simple is harder than complex.
